\begin{letter}{Editors\\Knowledge-Based Systems}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider our manuscript, ``SignalShap: Exact Ranking-Stage Source
Attribution for Hybrid Recommenders,''
for publication as a regular article in \emph{Knowledge-Based Systems}.

The manuscript distinguishes two quantities that are often conflated in
hybrid recommender analysis: allocation of ranking utility among architectural
sources and the effect of removing one source. It defines a five-player,
fixed-candidate cooperative game, enumerates all 32 coalitions exactly, and
compares Shapley allocation with ranking-stage and end-to-end removal effects
on three timestamped corpora. The contribution is methodological and
diagnostic rather than a state-of-the-art accuracy claim, and we believe its
combination of recommender systems, explainable AI, cooperative-game analysis,
and reproducible evaluation is well aligned with the journal.

The comparative evaluation covers eight attribution rules scored against the
observed cost of retiring each source: Shapley, Banzhaf, two binomial
semivalues, leave-one-in, exact forward selection, ranking-stage leave-one-out,
and an uninformative control. Because all eight are linear functionals of a
single enumerated coalition lattice, the comparison is exact rather than
sampled. Every coalition-averaging rule nominates the wrong source, four of
the five at Kendall $\tau = +0.20$ and the fifth at $+0.40$, while the two
rules that condition on the grand coalition reach $+1.00$ and $+0.80$. The
separation is therefore a property of averaging over coalitions, not an
artefact of Shapley's particular weights.

Two further results speak to robustness. First, exactness is load-bearing
rather than presentational: estimating the same values by permutation sampling
at 500 draws leaves a 95th-percentile error of $3.27\%$ of the grand-coalition
value, above both the ten-seed spread and the materiality threshold used
throughout, so a sampled estimator at a realistic budget could manufacture or
erase the effects we report. Second, the finding is not an artefact of holding
candidates fixed: when the characteristic function is rebuilt so that removing
a source also removes its retrieval contribution, matching the game to the
intervention, Shapley's agreement improves but still fails to identify the
source that is cheapest to retire, whereas ranking-stage leave-one-out
identifies it.

The submission includes the main manuscript, a separate Highlights file, and
self-contained supplementary material. The supplement contains the formal
proofs, analytic verification games, implementation and cost details,
robustness and provenance tables, the repeat-event audit, the estimand
comparison, and a negative result on attribution-derived fusion. Code and
derived artefacts are identified in the declarations and released under the
tag \texttt{kbs-submission-v2}.

This manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under
consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the manuscript and
the author order. The authors declare no competing interests. We request the
standard subscription route rather than gold open access. Use of AI tools is
disclosed in the manuscript in accordance with Elsevier's policy.

Thank you for your consideration.
