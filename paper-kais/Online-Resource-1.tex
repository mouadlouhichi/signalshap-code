\esmsection{S1}{Formal results: redundancy, monotonicity, and approximate symmetry}

Let $g_1$ and $g_2$ be \emph{exactly redundant} when, for every
$S\subseteq\G\setminus\{g_1,g_2\}$,
\[
v(S\cup\{g_1\})=v(S\cup\{g_2\})=v(S\cup\{g_1,g_2\}).
\]
This definition says that either copy supplies the same capability and the
second copy adds nothing once the first is present.

\begin{property}[Corrected redundancy statement]
Suppose $v(\varnothing)=0$, $v$ is monotone, $g_1$ and $g_2$ are exactly
redundant, and $v(\{g_1\})>0$. Then
\[
\LOO(g_1)=\LOO(g_2)=0,
\qquad
\varphi_{g_1}=\varphi_{g_2}\geq \frac{v(\{g_1\})}{|\G|}>0.
\]
\end{property}

\begin{proof}
Take $S=\G\setminus\{g_1,g_2\}$ in the redundancy identity. It gives
$v(\G)=v(\G\setminus\{g_1\})=v(\G\setminus\{g_2\})$, hence both LOO values
are zero. Symmetry gives $\varphi_{g_1}=\varphi_{g_2}$. In the Shapley sum for
$g_1$, every predecessor coalition containing $g_2$ has zero marginal by exact
redundancy. Every remaining marginal is non-negative by monotonicity. The empty
predecessor contributes
$\frac{0!(n-1)!}{n!}[v(\{g_1\})-v(\varnothing)]
=v(\{g_1\})/n$, which proves the bound.
\end{proof}

Monotonicity is necessary. The following three-player game satisfies exact
redundancy and $v(\{g_1\})>0$ but allocates negative value to both copies:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
S & \varnothing & g_1 & g_2 & g_3 & g_1g_2 & g_1g_3 & g_2g_3 & g_1g_2g_3\\
\hline
v(S) & 0 & 1 & 1 & 5 & 1 & 0.5 & 0.5 & 0.5
\end{array}
\]
Direct enumeration gives
$\varphi_{g_1}=\varphi_{g_2}=-5/12$ and $\varphi_{g_3}=4/3$, while both LOO
values for the copies are zero. The failure is caused by the non-monotone
marginals obtained when $g_3$ is combined with a copy.

\begin{lemma}[$\varepsilon$-symmetry]
If
\[
\max_{S\subseteq\G\setminus\{g_1,g_2\}}
\left|v(S\cup\{g_1\})-v(S\cup\{g_2\})\right|\leq\varepsilon,
\]
then $|\varphi_{g_1}-\varphi_{g_2}|\leq\varepsilon$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Pair the Shapley terms for $g_1$ and $g_2$ by a common coalition
$S\subseteq\G\setminus\{g_1,g_2\}$. Their difference is
\[
\varphi_{g_1}-\varphi_{g_2}
=\sum_S \frac{|S|!(n-|S|-2)!}{(n-1)!}
\left[v(S\cup\{g_1\})-v(S\cup\{g_2\})\right].
\]
For each coalition size $k$, the total coefficient is $1/(n-1)$; summing over
$k=0,\ldots,n-2$ gives total mass one. The triangle inequality therefore gives
the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\esmsection{S2}{Algorithm detail, Gram sufficient statistics, and cost}

The implementation fits all five source scorers once, builds one candidate set
$C_u$ per user, and forms the standardised matrix $Z_u$. For validation target
$\mathbf y_u^{\mathrm{val}}$, the sufficient statistics are
\[
A=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{eval}}}|C_u|^{-1}Z_u^\top Z_u,
\qquad
c=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{eval}}}|C_u|^{-1}
Z_u^\top\mathbf y_u^{\mathrm{val}}.
\]
For coalition $S$, the ridge head is obtained from the submatrix solve
\[
(A_{SS}+\lambda I)w^{(S)}=c_S.
\]
Thus the large user--candidate design is traversed once. Coalition fitting uses
only small matrices of order at most five. At $\lambda=0$, the implementation
uses the minimum-norm pseudoinverse and truncates singular values below
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}|S|\sigma_{\max}$; this is required for exact
duplicate columns.

With $n$ sources, Gram accumulation costs
$O(\sum_u |C_u|n^2)$, the $2^n$ small solves cost
$O(2^n n^3)$ in the loose worst case, and test scoring costs
$O(2^n\sum_u |C_u|n)$. The exponential term is therefore attached to small
matrix operations and rescoring, not repeated source fitting. Exact enumeration
is practical here because $n=5$.

\subsection*{S3.3 Semivalues and Grabisch--Roubens interactions}

The size-uniform semivalue is exactly Shapley because mass $1/n$ spread over
the $\binom{n-1}{k}$ predecessor coalitions of each size produces the Shapley
coefficient. It is therefore an implementation identity, not a comparator.
Banzhaf and the binomial semivalues at $q=0.25$ and $q=0.75$ are distinct.

\esmsection{S4}{Sampling-figure reconstruction audit}

The revised sampling figure uses the seed-42 MovieLens ranking-stage,
baseline-centred NDCG@10 game. The released exact Shapley, Banzhaf, two
binomial semivalues, five singleton values, ten pair-interaction indices, five
LOO values, grand value, and complete negative-marginal audit form 68 linear
constraints on the 32 coalition values. The system has rank 32 and maximum
constraint residual $8.4\times10^{-17}$. The recovered lattice reproduces 26
raw and 7 material monotonicity violations.

Permutation and KernelSHAP estimates use master seed 42, budgets
$M\in\{50,100,500,2000\}$, and 20 repeats. Error is
$\lVert\widehat\varphi-\varphi\rVert_\infty/|v(\G)|$. At $M=500$ the
permutation mean and 95th percentile are $1.51\%$ and $3.27\%$; KernelSHAP
gives $2.84\%$ and $4.73\%$. The reference lines are the $2.22\%$ ten-seed
spread and $1.93\%$ materiality threshold. The executable reconstruction and
all 32 recovered values are stored in
\texttt{scripts/rebuild\_sampling\_figure.py} and
\texttt{artefacts/fig2\_ndcg\_sampling.json}.

\esmsection{S5}{Attribution-derived fusion: negative result}

The exploratory fusion extension converted source attributions into
user/segment-specific fusion weights and compared them with one global ridge
head. This is not part of C1--C3 and is reported because the result is negative.
The hierarchical bootstrap over seeds then users gives a mean NDCG@10
difference of $0.000114$ with interval
$[-0.0000414,0.0002885]$, which includes zero. A separate equivalence test with
declared margin $0.002$ gives mean difference $0.0000286$, 90\% interval
$[-0.000427,0.000485]$, and $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}=6.4\times10^{-13}$.
Thus there is no resolvable improvement over the global head at the declared
scale; the attribution method is not presented as a fusion method.
